Study without burning out

Study smarter, not longer.

Most study advice is from people who don't remember being a student. Here's a short collection of techniques that are backed by research and actually work in real life.

Try it now

Focus timer (Pomodoro)

25 minutes focused, 5 minutes rest. After 4 rounds, take a longer break. It works because your brain focuses in short bursts, not marathons.

Techniques that actually work

1. Active recall

Close the book and try to write or say what you just read — from memory. You'll feel dumb at first. That feeling is the learning. Re-reading feels productive but barely moves information into long-term memory.

2. Spaced repetition

Review new material after 1 day, then 3, then 7, then 14. Short repeated exposure beats one giant session. Free apps like Anki are great for vocabulary and definitions.

3. The Feynman trick

Explain the topic out loud, in simple words, as if teaching a younger sibling. When you stumble, you've found the gap — go fix that spot only. Fast and brutal in a good way.

4. Interleaving

Don't spend 3 hours on one subject. Rotate: 25 min maths, 25 min English, 25 min bio. Your brain builds stronger connections by switching.

5. Practice in test conditions

Whatever your test looks like — closed book, timed, multiple choice — practise under those rules. Real transfer happens when the practice matches the performance.

Set up your workspace

  • Phone in another room. Or at minimum: inverted, silenced, in a drawer. The presence of a phone alone lowers focus even if you don't check it.
  • One tab rule. Only the tab you're studying in. Everything else is a break reward.
  • Water + snack ready. Fewer excuses to get up. Most "breaks" start with "I'll just grab…"
  • Lighting matters. Bright for focus, dim for review. Natural light is always a win.
  • Tell someone. "I'm studying until 5" to a parent or friend creates a gentle accountability loop.

When you just… can't start

Procrastination usually isn't laziness. It's your brain avoiding a task that feels uncertain, boring, or scary. Some tricks that help:

  • 2-minute rule: commit to 2 minutes only. Almost always you keep going.
  • Shrink the task. "Study biology" is too big. "Read pages 42–45" is actionable.
  • Start with the easiest step. Momentum beats motivation.
  • Body double. Study on a video call with a friend (cameras on, mics muted). Silent company works.
  • Tell your future self where you stopped. Leave a sticky note or comment so tomorrow's you doesn't re-spend energy remembering.
If procrastination never stops even after trying, it might be anxiety wearing a procrastination costume. See the stress techniques and consider talking to a counsellor.
