Study without burning out

Study smarter, not longer.

Most study advice is from people who don't remember being a student. Here's a short collection of techniques that are backed by research and actually work in real life.

Try it now

Focus timer (Pomodoro)

25 minutes focused, 5 minutes rest. After 4 rounds, take a longer break. It works because your brain focuses in short bursts, not marathons.

Techniques that actually work

1. Active recall

Close the book and try to write or say what you just read — from memory. Re-reading feels productive but barely moves information into long-term memory.

2. Spaced repetition

Review new material after 1 day, then 3, then 7, then 14. This is better than one big session. Free apps like Anki are great for vocabulary.

3. The Feynman trick

Explain the topic out loud, in simple words, as if teaching a younger sibling. When you stumble, go fix that part only.

4. Interleaving

Don't spend 3 hours on one subject. Change them: 25 min Maths, 25 min English, 25 min Science. Your brain builds stronger connections by switching.

Set up your workspace

  • Put your phone in another room. Or if you want it to be in your room, keep it silenced and in a drawer. The presence of a phone alone lowers focus even if you don't check it.
  • One tab rule.Keep only the tab you're studying with open. Everything else should be closed.
  • Water and snack ready. Make fewer excuses to get up. Most "breaks" start with "I'll just grab…"
  • Lighting matters. Use bright for focus, and dim for review. Natural light is always a win.
  • Tell someone. Tell "I'm studying until 5" to a parent or friend.

When you just… can't start

Procrastination usually isn't laziness. It's your brain avoiding a task that feels uncertain, boring, or scary. Some tricks that help:

  • 2-minute rule: Try to commit to 2 minutes only. Almost always you keep going.
  • Shrink the task. "Study biology" is too big. "Read pages 42–45" is actionable.
  • Start with the easiest step. Starting with small tasks helps to start and do other ones.
  • Tell your future self where you stopped. Leave a sticky note or comment so tomorrow's you don't use energy remembering what you were doing.
If procrastination never stops even after trying, it might be anxiety wearing a procrastination costume. See the stress techniques and consider talking to a counsellor.
