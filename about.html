About this project

Mind at Ease is my Grade 9 (MYP Year 4) Service & Action project. The goal is simple: build something genuinely useful that helps other students handle the pressure of school, exams, and everything else.

Why I made this

During the past year I noticed how many people around me were quietly stressed — classmates cramming at 1 AM, friends saying "I'm fine" when they clearly weren't, whole classrooms going silent the week before exams. Most of the advice teens see online is either too vague ("just meditate!") or written for adults.

I wanted a small, honest website that explains stress and anxiety in plain words and gives you things you can actually do — a breathing exercise, a timer, a place to write, a way to track how you feel, a list of real helplines. No accounts, no tracking, no noise.

What's on the site

Information

  • A detailed guide to stress and anxiety
  • Study tips that don't require being a genius
  • Austria-focused helplines + international ones

Interactive tools

  • Guided breathing animation (box breathing)
  • Mood check-in and weekly chart
  • Private journal with gentle prompts
  • Stress self-check quiz (7 questions)
  • Pomodoro focus timer
  • Light & dark theme toggle

How it's built

The website is built with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript on the front-end — no heavy frameworks — and a small FastAPI backend with MongoDB to save mood check-ins, journal entries, and quiz results. Everything is anonymous: when you first visit, your browser generates a random ID that only you have, stored in your own browser.

All the code is my own work for my MYP Service & Action project. The content was written by me based on information from the WHO, NHS, Mind UK, and YoungMinds, then reworded for a teenage audience.

How it links to MYP Global Contexts

This project sits inside the MYP Global Context Identities and relationships — exploring how young people cope with pressure, emotion, and self-image — and Personal and cultural expression through how students talk about (or avoid) mental health. It also connects to Fairness and development: access to mental health information shouldn't depend on knowing the right adult.

A note on limits

This website is a student project, not a medical service. If you're struggling, please use the real services on the Resources page — they're free, anonymous, and run by trained people who can help you much better than a website can.

Thank you

To the teachers who guided the Service & Action project, and to the friends who tested the website and gave feedback on what felt genuine and what didn't — thank you. If anything here helps someone feel a little less alone in a stressful moment, it was worth it.
