Support Resources

You're not alone. If you're struggling, these resources can help you get support quickly and safely.

How to Use These Resources

Different situations need different kinds of help. If you’re not sure where to start, use this guide:

  • Feeling stressed or overwhelmed? → Try breathing, apps, or talk to a friend
  • Feeling anxious often? → Use techniques + consider speaking to a counselor
  • Can’t cope or feel unsafe? → Contact a helpline immediately
You do not need to wait until things are “serious” to ask for help. Getting support early makes everything easier.

School & Personal Support

School Counselor

Your counselor is trained to help with stress, anxiety, academic pressure, and personal issues. Everything you share is usually confidential.

Best for: ongoing stress, anxiety, personal problems

Teachers

Teachers can help you adjust deadlines, explain topics again, or reduce workload pressure.

Best for: academic stress, deadlines

Friends & Family

Talking to someone you trust can reduce stress instantly. You don’t need solutions—just being heard helps.

Best for: emotional support

Everyday Self-Help

Sleep

7–9 hours helps your brain recover and reduces stress.

Exercise

Even short walks improve mood and reduce anxiety.

Talk

Sharing your thoughts reduces emotional pressure.

Time Management

Planning reduces last-minute stress.

Final Message

It is completely normal to feel stressed sometimes. You are not weak, and you are not alone.

The most important thing is to take action—small steps make a big difference.

If something feels too heavy to handle alone, reaching out is the strongest thing you can do.
